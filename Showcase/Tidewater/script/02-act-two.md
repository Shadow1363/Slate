# Act Two

## The storm night

= Juno learns the clock's rules: one night back for every turn, and the sea takes a price.

EXT. THE DROWNED MARKET - NIGHT {#s006 color=red}

Stalls on stilts over the water, lashed together with rope. Lanterns sway. The tide is rising fast.

Ellis crouches at the edge of the boards, fishing something out of the water with a hooked pole: a box. The same box.

Juno grabs his arm.

ELLIS
Get off! Who are you?

JUNO
Someone who needs you to go home.

ELLIS
I'm not done.

JUNO ^
You are. Tonight you are.

ELLIS ^
You sound like my sister.

~Oh the tide comes in and the tide goes out,
~and the keeper counts what the sea's about.

The song drifts from a stall. An OLD FISHWIFE sings it, mending a net, not looking at either of them.

INT. THE LIGHTHOUSE - LAMP ROOM - NIGHT {#s007 color=red}

1987. A YOUNGER TOBIAS (30s) tends the lamp. He turns as Juno bursts in, dripping.

TOBIAS
You're not the relief keeper.

JUNO
No. Listen to me. In an hour the market goes under, and Ellis Hale goes with it. You have to light the storm signal now.

TOBIAS
The signal's for ships, not boys.

JUNO
Then pretend he's a ship!

Tobias looks at the clock in her hands. Recognition. Fear.

TOBIAS
Where did you get that?

JUNO
Under your floor. Thirty-seven years from now.

A long silence. The wind screams around the glass.

TOBIAS
(quietly)
Then you already know how this ends. That clock doesn't give. It trades. Every night you take back, the sea takes something you didn't think to guard. Your name. Your face in a photograph. The way your mother said goodnight. It took my brother's voice from me, one word at a time, until I couldn't remember if he'd ever laughed. I buried that thing so nobody would ever wind it again, and I'd thank you to put it back in the ground and walk away from this lighthouse before the tide decides what you owe.

===

EXT. SALTMARROW CLIFFS - NIGHT {#s008}

Juno runs the cliff path, the beam sweeping over her. Below, the Drowned Market's lanterns go out one by one as the water takes them.

She stops at the edge. Winds the clock. Twice.
